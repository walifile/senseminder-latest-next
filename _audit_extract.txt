sensepc.com Website Audit and Growth Report 
Executive summary 
sensepc.com positions itself around cloud desktops / virtual desktops (Desktop‑as‑a‑Service) rather 
than a traditional “PC builder” or local IT storefront, and the closest mainstream comparators are 
Windows 365 and Amazon WorkSpaces (plus enterprise brokers such as Citrix DaaS). 
 
A key technical blocker for this review is that, in this environment, the site could not be rendered or 
text‑extracted (the crawler returned no readable document lines for the homepage). That typically 
indicates a JavaScript‑rendered site (SPA) without reliably accessible server ‑rendered HTML, or anti ‑bot 
delivery that prevents automated fetching. This has direct consequences for SEO (indexing), 
accessibility, and performance diagnostics , and it also constrained the ability to quote exact 
headings, CTAs, and copy from most pages.  
 
Despite those constraints, several high‑confidence conclusions and actions still stand: 
 
• Treat “renderability + indexability” as a top‑priority risk. If primary content is not present in 
initial HTML, search engines and accessibility tools may struggle; your marketing pages should 
be SSR/pre‑rendered and readable without client‑side execution (at least for key sections and 
metadata). 
• Pricing clarity and conversion flow are competitive differentiators in DaaS. Competitors 
present highly structured pricing tables and predictable plans (e.g., Windows 365’s per‑user/ 
month table; WorkSpaces highlights monthly vs hourly metering). 
• A stronger “why us” story (trust, security, compliance,  latency footprint, support SLAs, 
onboarding) is required to compete in this category, where buyers compare risk as much as 
specs. Competitor pricing pages explicitly frame cost control and flexibility; you should mirror 
that narrative and strengthen proof.  
• Immediate roadmap: (1) ensure crawlable/SSR content and basic technical SEO fundamentals, 
(2) optimise “Plans” and “Get started” funnel, (3) build an education moat (use cases + 
comparison + FAQs), (4) instrument analytics and conversion tracking (GA4/GTM + events) and 
iterate. 
Assumptions (explicit): competitors were selected in the DaaS / Cloud PC space due to the brand’s 
implied offering and public trademark classification  describing SaaS for virtual desktops/cloud storage/ 
file management.

Information architecture and navigation risks 
 
If sensepc.com is primarily client ‑rendered (as the crawler behaviour suggests), there are three IA risks 
that commonly appear:  
 
1. Navigation exists visually but is not consistently discoverable to crawlers (links may be 
injected after JS executes), which weakens index coverage and internal PageRank flow. 
2. Deep pages are “orphaned” from search: pricing, support and legal pages often become 
accessible only through footer components that are also JS ‑injected. 
3. Sitemap/robots governance becomes critical: in JS apps, a clean sitemap.xml can compensate 
for weaker crawl discovery —if it is accessible and maintained.  
Because robots.txt and sitemap.xml could not be fetched in this environment, confirm quickly on your 
side whether: - robots.txt  exists and links to sitemap.xml  - sitemap.xml  (or sitemap index) lists canonical, 
200‑status URLs for all marketing pages and major product flows  
 
Content, UX and accessibility review 
Messaging clarity and value proposition 
 
In DaaS, the minimum “above the fold” messaging  that converts usually includes: 
 
• Who it is for (remote teams, contractors,  students, creatives,  developers) 
• What you get (a Windows Cloud PC / cloud desktop you can access from anywhere) 
• Why it’s safe (security posture and admin controls) 
• Why it’s better (cost predictability,  performance,  setup speed, support) 
 
Competitors explicitly surface plan structure and the promise of budget predictability and flexibility on 
pricing pages (which function as both product documentation and conversion drivers). For example, 
Amazon WorkSpaces frames pricing as “flexible and cost ‑effective” and highlights subscription vs hourly 
metering. 
 
Windows 365 pricing pages are extremely literal: a specs table (vCPU/RAM/storage) with monthly 
subscription rows —this reduces buyer uncertainty a t the point of decision.  
 
Recommendation: ensure your homepage hero and the first scroll deliver the same clarity and 
reassurance, with a “Start trial / Get a Cloud PC” CTA and a secondary “See plans” CTA.

Calls‑to‑action and conversion flow 
 
Because the site could not be rendered here, CTAs cannot be enumerated, but the recommended  
conversion flow for this category is stable:  
 
• Primary CTA: Get started (plan selection → account creation → provisioning) 
• Secondary CTA: See pricing (transparent plan table) 
• Trust CTA: Security & compliance (policies, encryption, data region, SSO/MFA, audit logs) 
• Support CTA: Talk to sales / contact specialist for business buyers (mirroring WorkSpaces’ 
“Contact a specialist”). 
Visual design and UX heuristics 
 
Without page rendering, visual critique is necessarily indirect, but you can still validate these 
high‑impact UX patterns immediately:  
 
• Plan comparison layout: table + “recommended” plan badge + clear monthly cost + included 
features + add‑ons. 
• Reduced cognitive  load: in DaaS, too many hardware ‑like spec options without “good/better/ 
best” framing increases drop ‑off; consider 3 –5 curated tiers.  
• Onboarding  reassurance:  provisioning  time expectations,  what device you need, and “what 
happens after purchase”.  
 
Accessibility (WCAG basics) 
 
Given the likely JS dependency  observed, prioritise two accessibility angles: 
 
• Semantic  HTML and headings:  ensure a single H1 per page and logical heading order, because 
screen readers and SEO both rely on it.  
• Keyboard navigation and focus handling: SPAs often regress on focus trapping, skip links, and 
modal behaviour.  
Mobile friendliness: note that Google retired the public Mobile Friendly Test and mobile usability report 
(December 1, 2023 up date). Therefore, your audit baseline should be Lighthouse mobile emulation + 
Core Web Vitals field data (Search Console / CrUX) when available.

Technical SEO essentials to verify

Core technical checks: 
 
• Title tags & meta descriptions per marketing page (unique, intent‑matched). 
• Canonical  tags (especially  if you have marketing variants or tracking params). 
• Indexability: correct use of robots meta and HTTP headers; avoid accidental  on

• Robots.txt and sitemap.xml: must exist, be readable, and list canonical pages.

On‑page SEO and content depth 
 
In DaaS, the highest ‑value SEO pages tend to be: 
 
• “Cloud PC / Cloud Desktop” landing page targeting non‑brand intent 
• “Plans & pricing” 
• “Use cases” pages (remote work, call centres, dev environments,  education) 
• “Comparison”  pages (vs Windows 365, vs WorkSpaces)  with objective differences 
• Support/FAQ pages that answer provisioning, latency, security, and licensing questions 
 
The Justia trademark listing associated with SENSEPC explicitly references  SaaS for virtual desktops/

Performance and Core Web Vitals 
 
No first ‑party PSI/Lighthouse numeric scores could be captured here. Nonetheless, the risk model is 
clear: JS ‑heavy pages frequently accrue large JS payloads and longer main ‑thread blocking, which 
pushes down LCP/INP and harms mobile conversion.  The PageSpeed  Insights tooling is explicitly 
geared to return “PageSpeed scores… and suggestions” based on Lighthouse audits.  
 
Actionable performance checklist (implementation ‑ready): - Pre‑render marketing pages (SSR/SSG) 
and minimise JS required for first paint. - Optimise and preconnect fonts; avoid layout shifts by  
reserving dimensions. - Compress hero media and ensure modern image  formats where supported. - 
Add caching headers and consider a CDN for static assets.  
 
Security and HTTPS posture 
 
Direct security header scans (e.g., securityheaders.com output) and certificate transparency queries 
(crt.sh) were not available for sensepc. com in this session, but these are baseline expectations for a 
product that sells remote desktops:  
 
• Enforce HTTPS site‑wide (HSTS recommended). 
• Avoid mixed content (HTTP assets on HTTPS pages). 
• Deploy modern security headers (CSP, X‑Content ‑Type‑Options, frame protections, 
Permissions‑Policy). 
• Publish clear privacy/terms/cookie documentation and cookie controls. 
 
For header coverage, scanners typically check for CSP, HSTS, X‑Frame‑Options and related modern 
headers.

(Standard → Premium Plus), even when detail is thin, which signals segmentation by needs.
